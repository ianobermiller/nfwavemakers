### Speech: What an interp is

We started our lessons on interpretive speeches.

Interps are speeches in which the speaker acts out a story for an audience.

The five attributes of an interp: content, organization, characterization, blocking, and impact.

We need to understand the purpose behind our interp. Do we want to inform, persuade, inspire, connect, entertain, or some combination of those?

Jesus used stories to make impactful and memorable points.

Groups also began a retelling of *The Three Little Pigs*.

### Speech homework

**Interps (everyone)**

- Make your own “Name That Story.”
- Make a 20-story inventory. Include title, author, genre, and why you remember the story. These are not necessarily stories that you want to do an interp on. This is a list of stories that you enjoy and/or remember. We’ll talk about how to choose good source material. This homework is just making an inventory of stories or books.

If your group didn’t get to perform your version of *The Three Little Pigs*, prepare it. We will start with those at the next club meeting.

**Platforms.** If you haven’t already, brainstorm possible topics for Informative, Persuasive, Digital, or After Dinner. Then narrow your topics down to your top 5 possibilities and write objective statements and rationales for each. Bring your answers to club.

Debate requires hard work. Take time to really study and research these briefs. If you haven’t ever done this before and have questions, reach out to Coach Shannon. I recommend uploading your briefs to your choice of AI and asking it to explain the brief to you like you are a 12 year old. This helps you get to the foundation of the case in a quick and easy way and then allows you to layer on the harder to grasp concepts.
